Corollary 4.1.16.

Let \(A\) be a square matrix. If the rows or columns of \(A\) are linearly dependent, then \(\det(A)=0\text{.}\)
🔗
🔗

Proof.
If the columns of \(A\) are linearly dependent, then \(A\) is not invertible by condition 4 of the Theorem 1 of Section 3.6. Suppose now that the rows of \(A\) are linearly dependent. If \(r_1,r_2,\ldots,r_n\) are the rows of \(A\text{,}\) then one of the rows is in the span of the others, so we have an equation like
\begin{gather*} r_2 = 3r_1 - r_3 + 2r_4\text{.} \end{gather*}
If we perform the following row operations on \(A\text{:}\)
\begin{gather*} R_2 = R_2 - 3R_1;\quad R_2 = R_2 + R_3;\quad R_2 = R_2 - 2R_4 \end{gather*}
then the second row of the resulting matrix is zero. Hence \(A\) is not invertible in this case either.
🔗
Alternatively, if the rows of \(A\) are linearly dependent, then one can combine condition 4 of the Theorem 1 of Section 3.6 and the Proposition 25 below to conclude that \(\det(A)=0\text{.}\)
