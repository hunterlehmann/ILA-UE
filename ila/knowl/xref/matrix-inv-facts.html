Fact 3.5.4 Facts about invertible matrices.

Let \(A\) and \(B\) be invertible \(n\times n\) matrices.
  1. \(A\inv\) is invertible, and its inverse is \((A\inv){}\inv = A.\)
    🔗
    🔗
  2. \(AB\) is invertible, and its inverse is \((AB)\inv = B\inv A\inv\) (note the order).
    🔗
    🔗
🔗
🔗

Proof.
  1. The equations \(AA^{-1}=I_n\) and \(A^{-1}A = I_n\) at the same time exhibit \(A^{-1}\) as the inverse of \(A\) and \(A\) as the inverse of \(A^{-1}.\)
    🔗
    🔗
  2. We compute
    \begin{gather*} (B\inv A\inv)AB = B\inv(A\inv A)B = B\inv I_n B = B\inv B = I_n\text{.} \end{gather*}
    Here we used the associativity of matrix multiplication and the fact that \(I_n B = B\text{.}\) This shows that \(B\inv A\inv\) is the inverse of \(AB\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
