Interactive 5.3.12 Another matrix similar to \(B\).

Consider the matrices
\begin{align*} A' = \frac 15\begin{pmatrix} -8 \amp -9 \\ 6 \amp 13 \end{pmatrix} \qquad B = \begin{pmatrix} 2 \amp 0 \\ 0 \amp -1 \end{pmatrix} \qquad C' = \frac 12\begin{pmatrix} -1 \amp -3 \\ 2 \amp 1 \end{pmatrix}\text{.} \end{align*}
Then \(A' = C'B(C')\inv\text{,}\) as one can verify. Let \(v_1'=\frac 12{-1\choose 2}\) and \(v_2' = \frac 12{-3\choose 1}\text{,}\) the columns of \(C'\text{,}\) and let \(\cB' = \{v_1',v_2'\}\text{.}\) Then \(A'\) does the same thing as \(B\text{,}\) as in Example 10, except \(A'\) uses the \(\cB'\)-coordinate system. In other words:
  • \(B\) scales the \(e_1\)-direction by \(2\) and the \(e_2\)-direction by \(-1\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
  • \(A'\) scales the \(v_1'\)-direction by \(2\) and the \(v_2'\)-direction by \(-1\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
