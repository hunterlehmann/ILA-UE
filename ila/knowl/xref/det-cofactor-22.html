Example 4.2.6 The determinant of a \(2\times 2\) matrix.

Let us compute (again) the determinant of a general \(2\times2\) matrix
\begin{align*} A=\begin{pmatrix} a \amp b \\ c \amp d \end{pmatrix}\text{.} \end{align*}

The minors are all \(1\times 1\) matrices. As we have seen that the determinant of a \(1\times1\) matrix is just the number inside of it, the cofactors are therefore
\begin{align*} C_{11} \amp= {+\det(A_{11}) = d} \amp C_{12} \amp= {-\det(A_{12}) = -c}\\ C_{21} \amp= {-\det(A_{21}) = -b} \amp C_{22} \amp= {+\det(A_{22}) = a} \end{align*}
Expanding cofactors along the first column, we find that
\begin{gather*} \det(A)=aC_{11}+cC_{21} = ad - bc\text{,} \end{gather*}
which agrees with the formulas in Definition 5 of Section 3.5 and Example 9 of Section 4.1.
