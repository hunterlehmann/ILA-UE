Example 5.3.14 A matrix similar to a rotation matrix.

Consider the matrices
\begin{align*} A = \frac 16\begin{pmatrix} 7 \amp -17 \\ 5 \amp -7 \end{pmatrix} \qquad B = \begin{pmatrix} 0 \amp -1 \\ 1 \amp 0 \end{pmatrix} \qquad C = \begin{pmatrix} 2 \amp -1/2 \\ 1 \amp 1/2 \end{pmatrix}\text{.} \end{align*}
One can verify that \(A = CBC\inv.\) Let \(v_1 = {2\choose 1}\) and \(v_2 = \frac 12{-1\choose 1}\text{,}\) the columns of \(C\text{,}\) and let \(\cB = \{v_1,v_2\}\text{,}\) a basis of \(\R^2\text{.}\)
🔗
The matrix \(B\) rotates the plane counterclockwise by \(90^\circ\text{.}\) \def\theo{\includegraphics[width=6cm]{theo10.jpg}} \begin{tikzpicture}[scale=.8, thin border nodes] \node[transform shape] at (0, 0) {\theo}; \draw[seq-violet, opacity=1, vector] (0,0) -- (1,0) node[right] {$e_1$}; \draw[seq-blue, opacity=1, vector] (0,0) -- (0,1) node[above] {$e_2$}; \point at (0, 0); \begin{scope}[xshift=10cm] \clip (-3, -3) rectangle (3, 3); \begin{scope}[rotate=90] \node[transform shape] at (0, 0) {\theo}; \draw[seq-violet, opacity=1, vector] (0,0) -- (1, 0) node[above] {$Be_1$}; \draw[seq-blue, opacity=1, vector] (0,0) -- (0, 1) node[left] {$Be_2$}; \end{scope} \point at (0, 0); \end{scope} \draw (-3, -3) rectangle (3, 3); \draw[xshift=10cm] (-3, -3) rectangle (3, 3); \draw[->, shorten=2mm, thick] (3, 1) to[bend left, "$B$" above=.5mm] (7, 1); \end{tikzpicture} To compute \(Ax\text{,}\) first we multiply by \(C\inv\) to find the \(\cB\)-coordinates of \(x\text{,}\) then we multiply by \(B\text{,}\) then we multiply by \(C\) again. For instance, let \(\textcolor{seq-green}x = \frac 32{1\choose 1}\text{.}\)
  1. We see from the \(\cB\)-coordinate grid below that \(\textcolor{seq-green}x = \textcolor{seq-violet}{v_1} + \mhb{v_2}\text{.}\) Therefore, \(C\inv\textcolor{seq-green}x = \mhg{[x]_\cB} = {1\choose 1}.\)
    🔗
    🔗
  2. Multiplying by \(B\) rotates by \(90^\circ\text{:}\) \(\mh{B[x]_\cB} = {-1\choose 1}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
  3. Interpreting \({-1\choose 1}\) as a \(\cB\)-coordinate vector, we multiply by \(C\) to get
    \begin{gather*} \mh{Ax} = C\begin{pmatrix} -1 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix} = -\textcolor{seq-violet}{v_1} + \mhb{v_2} = \frac 12\begin{pmatrix} -5 \\ -1 \end{pmatrix}\text{.} \end{gather*}
    Of course, this vector lies at \((-1, 1)\) on the \(\cB\)-coordinate grid.
    🔗
    🔗
🔗

🔗
Now let \(\textcolor{seq-green}x = \frac 12{-1\choose -2}\text{.}\)
  1. We see from the \(\cB\)-coordinate grid that \(\textcolor{seq-green}x = -\frac 12\textcolor{seq-violet}{v_1} - \mhb{v_2}\text{.}\) Therefore, \(C\inv\textcolor{seq-green}x = \mhg{[x]_\cB} = {-1/2\choose -1}.\)
    🔗
    🔗
  2. Multiplying by \(B\) rotates by \(90^\circ\text{:}\) \(\mh{B[x]_\cB} = {1\choose -1/2}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
  3. Interpreting \({1\choose -1/2}\) as a \(\cB\)-coordinate vector, we multiply by \(C\) to get
    \begin{gather*} \mh{Ax} = C\begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ -1/2 \end{pmatrix} = \textcolor{seq-violet}{v_1} - \frac 12\mhb{v_2} = \frac 14\begin{pmatrix} 9 \\ 3 \end{pmatrix}\text{.} \end{gather*}
    This vector lies at \((1, -\frac 12)\) on the \(\cB\)-coordinate grid.
    🔗
    🔗
🔗

🔗
To summarize:
  • \(B\) rotates counterclockwise around the circle centered at the origin and passing through \(e_1\) and \(e_2\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
  • \(A\) rotates counterclockwise around the ellipse centered at the origin and passing through \(v_1\) and \(v_2\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
