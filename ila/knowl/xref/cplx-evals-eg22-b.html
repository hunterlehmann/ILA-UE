Example 5.5.6 A \(2\times 2\) matrix, the easy way.

Find the complex eigenvalues and eigenvectors of the matrix
\begin{align*} A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 \amp -1 \\ 1 \amp 1 \end{pmatrix}\text{.} \end{align*}
🔗

Solution.

Since the characteristic polynomial of a \(2\times 2\) matrix \(A\) is \(f(\lambda) = \lambda^2-\Tr(A)\lambda + \det(A)\text{,}\) its roots are
\begin{gather*} \lambda = \frac{\Tr(A)\pm\sqrt{\Tr(A)^2-4\det(A)}}2 = \frac{2\pm\sqrt{4-8}}2 = 1\pm i\text{.} \end{gather*}
To find an eigenvector with eigenvalue \(1+i\text{,}\) we compute
\begin{align*} A - (1+i)I_2 = \begin{pmatrix} -i \amp -1 \\ \star \amp \star \end{pmatrix} \;\xrightarrow{\text{eigenvector}}\; v_1 = \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ -i \end{pmatrix}\text{.} \end{align*}
The eigenvector for the conjugate eigenvalue is the complex conjugate:
\begin{gather*} v_2 = \bar v_1 = \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ i \end{pmatrix}\text{.} \end{gather*}
