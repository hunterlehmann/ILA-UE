Example 3.4.13 Non-commutative composition of transformations.

Define matrix transformations \(T,U\colon\R^2\to\R^2\) by
\begin{align*} T(x) = \begin{pmatrix} 1 \amp 1 \\ 0 \amp 1 \end{pmatrix}x \sptxt{and} U(x) = \begin{pmatrix} 1 \amp 0 \\ 1 \amp 1 \end{pmatrix}x\text{.} \end{align*}
Geometrically, \(T\) is a shear in the \(x\)-direction, and \(U\) is a shear in the \(Y\)-direction. We evaluate
\begin{gather*} T\circ U\begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix} = T\begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 2 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix} \end{gather*}
and
\begin{gather*} U\circ T\begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix} = U\begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix}\text{.} \end{gather*}
Since \(T\circ U\) and \(U\circ T\) have different outputs for the input vector \(1\choose 0\text{,}\) they are different transformations. (See Example 23.)
