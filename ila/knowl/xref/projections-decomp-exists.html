Theorem 6.3.2 Orthogonal decomposition.

Let \(W\) be a subspace of \(\R^n\) and let \(x\) be a vector in \(\R^n\text{.}\) Then we can write \(x\) uniquely as
\begin{gather*} x = x_W + x_{W^\perp} \end{gather*}
where \(x_W\) is the closest vector to \(x\) on \(W\) and \(x_{W^\perp}\) is in \(W^\perp\text{.}\)
🔗
🔗

Proof.
Let \(m = \dim(W)\text{,}\) so \(n-m = \dim(W^\perp)\) by Fact 16 of Section 6.2. Let \(v_1,v_2,\ldots,v_m\) be a basis for \(W\) and let \(v_{m+1},v_{m+2},\ldots,v_n\) be a basis for \(W^\perp\text{.}\) We showed in the proof of Fact 16 of Section 6.2 that \(\{v_1,v_2,\ldots,v_m,v_{m+1},v_{m+2},\ldots,v_n\}\) is linearly independent, so it forms a basis for \(\R^n\text{.}\) Therefore, we can write
\begin{gather*} x = (c_1v_1 + \cdots + c_mv_m) + (c_{m+1}v_{m+1} + \cdots + c_nv_n) = x_W + x_{W^\perp}\text{,} \end{gather*}
where \(x_W = c_1v_1 + \cdots + c_mv_m\) and \(x_{W^\perp} = c_{m+1}v_{m+1} + \cdots + c_nv_n\text{.}\) Since \(x_{W^\perp}\) is orthogonal to \(W\text{,}\) the vector \(x_W\) is the closest vector to \(x\) on \(W\text{,}\) so this proves that such a decomposition exists.
🔗
As for uniqueness, suppose that
\begin{gather*} x = x_W + x_{W^\perp} = y_W + y_{W^\perp} \end{gather*}
for \(x_W,y_W\) in \(W\) and \(x_{W^\perp},y_{W^\perp}\) in \(W^\perp\text{.}\) Rearranging gives
\begin{gather*} x_W - y_W = y_{W^\perp} - x_{W^\perp}\text{.} \end{gather*}
Since \(W\) and \(W^\perp\) are subspaces, the left side of the equation is in \(W\) and the right side is in \(W^\perp\text{.}\) Therefore, \(x_W-y_W\) is in \(W\) and in \(W^\perp\text{,}\) so it is orthogonal to itself, which implies \(x_W-y_W=0\text{.}\) Hence \(x_W = y_W\) and \(x_{W^\perp} = y_{W^\perp}\text{,}\) which proves uniqueness.
