Theorem 3.2.5 One-to-one matrix transformations.

Let \(A\) be an \(m\times n\) matrix, and let \(T(x)=Ax\) be the associated matrix transformation. The following statements are equivalent:
  1. \(T\) is one-to-one.
    🔗
    🔗
  2. For every \(b\) in \(\R^m\text{,}\) the equation \(T(x)=b\) has at most one solution.
    🔗
    🔗
  3. For every \(b\) in \(\R^m\text{,}\) the equation \(Ax=b\) has a unique solution or is inconsistent.
    🔗
    🔗
  4. \(Ax=0\) has only the trivial solution.
    🔗
    🔗
  5. The columns of \(A\) are linearly independent.
    🔗
    🔗
  6. \(A\) has a pivot in every column.
    🔗
    🔗
  7. The range of \(T\) has dimension \(n\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
🔗
🔗

Proof.
Statements 1, 2, and 3 are translations of each other. The equivalence of 3 and 4 follows from Note : if \(Ax=0\) has only one solution, then \(Ax=b\) has only one solution as well, or it is inconsistent. The equivalence of 4, 5, and 6 is a consequence of Recipe 9 of Section 2.5, and the equivalence of 6 and 7 follows from the fact that the rank of a matrix is equal to the number of columns with pivots.
