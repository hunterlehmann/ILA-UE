Definition 3.4.36.

  • The sum of two \(m\times n\) matrices is the matrix obtained by summing the entries of \(A\) and \(B\) individually:
    \begin{align*} \begin{pmatrix} a_{11} \amp a_{12} \amp a_{13} \\ a_{21} \amp a_{22} \amp a_{23} \end{pmatrix} + \begin{pmatrix} b_{11} \amp b_{12} \amp b_{13} \\ b_{21} \amp b_{22} \amp b_{23} \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} a_{11}+b_{11} \amp a_{12}+b_{12} \amp a_{13}+b_{13} \\ a_{21}+b_{21} \amp a_{22}+b_{22} \amp a_{23}+b_{23} \end{pmatrix} \end{align*}
    In other words, the \(i,j\) entry of \(A+B\) is the sum of the \(i,j\) entries of \(A\) and \(B\text{.}\) Note that addition of matrices is only defined when both matrices have the same size.

  • The scalar product of a scalar \(c\) with a matrix \(A\) is obtained by scaling all entries of \(A\) by \(c\text{:}\)
    \begin{align*} \def\mh{\textcolor{red}} \mh{c\begin{pmatrix}} a_{11} \amp a_{12} \amp a_{13} \\ a_{21} \amp a_{22} \amp a_{23} \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} \amp ca_{11} \amp \amp ca_{12} \amp \amp ca_{13} \\ \amp ca_{21} \amp \amp ca_{22} \amp \amp ca_{23} \end{pmatrix} \end{align*}
    In other words, the \(i,j\) entry of \(cA\) is \(c\) times the \(i,j\) entry of \(A\text{.}\)
