Example 5.4.31 Eigenvalues \(|\lambda_1| \lt 1,\,|\lambda_2| \lt 1\).

Describe how the matrix
\begin{align*} A' = \frac 1{30}\begin{pmatrix} 12 \amp 2 \\ 3 \amp 13 \end{pmatrix} \end{align*}
acts on the plane.
🔗

Solution.

This is the inverse of the matrix \(A\) from Example 29. In that example, we found \(A = CDC\inv\) for
\begin{align*} C = \begin{pmatrix} 2/3 \amp -1 \\ 1 \amp 1 \end{pmatrix} \qquad D = \begin{pmatrix} 2 \amp 0 \\ 0 \amp 3 \end{pmatrix}\text{.} \end{align*}
Therefore, remembering that taking inverses reverses the order of multiplication, we have
\begin{align*} A' = A\inv = (CDC\inv)\inv = (C\inv)\inv D\inv C\inv = C\begin{pmatrix} 1/2 \amp 0 \\ 0 \amp 1/3 \end{pmatrix}C\inv\text{.} \end{align*}
The diagonal matrix \(D\inv\) does the opposite of what \(D\) does: it scales the \(x\)-coordinate by \(1/2\) and the \(y\)-coordinate by \(1/3\text{.}\) Therefore, it moves vectors closer to both coordinate axes, but faster in the \(y\)-direction. The matrix \(A'\) does the same thing, but with respect to the \(v_1,v_2\)-coordinate system.
