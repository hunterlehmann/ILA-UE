Theorem 3.6.1.

Let \(A\) be an \(n\times n\) matrix, and let \(T\colon\R^n\to\R^n\) be the matrix transformation \(T(x) = Ax\text{.}\) The following statements are equivalent:
  1. \(A\) is invertible.
    🔗
    🔗
  2. \(A\) has \(n\) pivots.
    🔗
    🔗
  3. \(\Nul(A) = \{0\}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
  4. The columns of \(A\) are linearly independent.
    🔗
    🔗
  5. The columns of \(A\) span \(\R^n\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
  6. \(Ax=b\) has a unique solution for each \(b\) in \(\R^n\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
  7. \(T\) is invertible.
    🔗
    🔗
  8. \(T\) is one-to-one.
    🔗
    🔗
  9. \(T\) is onto.
    🔗
    🔗
🔗
🔗

Proof.
\((1\iff 2)\text{:}\) The matrix \(A\) has \(n\) pivots if and only if its reduced row echelon form is the identity matrix \(I_n\text{.}\) This happens exactly when the Theorem 8 of Section 3.5 to compute the inverse succeeds.
🔗
\((2\iff 3)\text{:}\) The null space of a matrix is \(\{0\}\) if and only if the matrix has no free variables, which means that every column is a pivot column, which means \(A\) has \(n\) pivots. See Recipe 18 of Section 2.6.
🔗
\((2\iff 4,\,2\iff 5)\text{:}\) These follow from Recipe 9 of Section 2.5 and Theorem 16 of Section 2.3, respectively, since \(A\) has \(n\) pivots if and only if has a pivot in every row/column.
🔗
\((4+5\iff 6)\text{:}\) We know \(Ax=b\) has at least one solution for every \(b\) if and only if the columns of \(A\) span \(\R^n\) by Theorem 19 of Section 3.2, and \(Ax=b\) has at most one solution for every \(b\) if and only if the columns of \(A\) are linearly independent by Theorem 5 of Section 3.2. Hence \(Ax=b\) has exactly one solution for every \(b\) if and only if its columns are linearly independent and span \(\R^n\text{.}\)
🔗
\((1\iff 7)\text{:}\) This is the content of Theorem 29 of Section 3.5.
🔗
\((7\implies 8+9)\text{:}\) See Proposition 26 of Section 3.5.
🔗
\((8\iff 4,\,9\iff 5)\text{:}\) See Theorem 5 of Section 3.2 and Theorem 19 of Section 3.2.
