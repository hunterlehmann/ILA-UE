Writing a subspace as a column space or a null space.

A subspace can be given to you in many different forms. In practice, computations involving subspaces are much easier if your subspace is the column space or null space of a matrix. The simplest example of such a computation is finding a spanning set: a column space is by definition the span of the columns of a matrix, and we showed above how to compute a spanning set for a null space using parametric vector form. For this reason, it is useful to rewrite a subspace as a column space or a null space before trying to answer questions about it.
🔗
When asking questions about a subspace, it is usually best to rewrite the subspace as a column space or a null space.
🔗
🔗
This also applies to the question “is my subset a subspace?” If your subset is a column space or null space of a matrix, then the answer is yes.
🔗

Example 2.6.21.

Let
\begin{gather*} V = \left\{ \begin{pmatrix} a \\ b \end{pmatrix} \text{ in } \R^2 \bigm| 2a = 3b\right\} \end{gather*}
be the subset of a previous Example 12. The subset \(V\) is exactly the solution set of the homogeneous equation \(2x - 3y = 0\text{.}\) Therefore,
\begin{align*} V = \Nul\begin{pmatrix} 2 \amp -3 \end{pmatrix}\text{.} \end{align*}
In particular, it is a subspace. The reduced row echelon form of \(\begin{pmatrix} 2 \amp -3 \end{pmatrix}\) is \(\begin{pmatrix} 1 \amp -3/2 \end{pmatrix}\text{,}\) so the parametric form of \(V\) is \(x = 3/2y\text{,}\) so the parametric vector form is \({x\choose y} = y{3/2\choose 1},\) and hence \(\bigl\{{3/2\choose 1}\bigr\}\) spans \(V\text{.}\)
🔗
🔗

Example 2.6.22.

Let \(V\) be the plane in \(\R^3\) defined by
\begin{gather*} V = \left\{\begin{pmatrix} 2x+y \\ x-y \\ 3x-2y \end{pmatrix}\bigm| x,y \text{ are in } \R \right\}\text{.} \end{gather*}
Write \(V\) as the column space of a matrix.
🔗

Solution.

Since
\begin{gather*} \begin{pmatrix} 2x+y \\ x-y \\ 3x-2y \end{pmatrix} = x\begin{pmatrix} 2 \\ 1 \\ 3 \end{pmatrix} + y\begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ -1 \\ -2 \end{pmatrix}\text{,} \end{gather*}
we notice that \(V\) is exactly the span of the vectors
\begin{gather*} \begin{pmatrix} 2 \\ 1 \\ 3 \end{pmatrix} \sptxt{and} \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ -1 \\ -2 \end{pmatrix}\text{.} \end{gather*}
Hence
\begin{align*} V = \Col\begin{pmatrix} 2 \amp 1 \\ 1 \amp -1 \\ 3 \amp -2 \end{pmatrix}\text{.} \end{align*}
