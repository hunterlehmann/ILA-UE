Example 2.4.5 Parametric Vector Form (homogeneous case).

Consider the following matrix in reduced row echelon form:
\begin{align*} A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 \amp 0 \amp -8 \amp -7 \\ 0 \amp 1 \amp 4 \amp 3 \\ 0 \amp 0 \amp 0 \amp 0 \end{pmatrix}\text{.} \end{align*}
The matrix equation \(Ax=0\) corresponds to the system of equations
\begin{align*} \left\{\begin{alignedat}{5} x_1 \amp {} \amp {} \amp {} \amp 8x_3 \amp {}-{} \amp 7x_4 \amp {}={} \amp 0 \amp {} \\ {} \amp {} \amp x_2 \amp {}+{} \amp 4x_3 \amp {}+{} \amp 3x_4 \amp {}={} \amp 0 \amp . \end{alignedat}\right. \end{align*}
We can write the parametric form as follows:
\begin{align*} \left\{\begin{alignedat}{2} x_1 \amp {}={} \amp 8x_3 + 7x_4 \amp {} \\ x_2 \amp {}={} \amp -4x_3 - 3x_4 \amp {} \\ x_3 \amp {}={} \amp x_3 \amp {} \\ x_4 \amp {}={} \amp x_4 \amp . \end{alignedat}\right. \end{align*}
We wrote the redundant equations \(x_3=x_3\) and \(x_4=x_4\) in order to turn the above system into a vector equation:
\begin{gather*} x = \begin{pmatrix} x_1 \\ x_2 \\ x_3 \\ x_4 \end{pmatrix} = x_3\begin{pmatrix} 8 \\ -4 \\ 1 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix} + x_4\begin{pmatrix} 7 \\ -3 \\ 0 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix}\text{.} \end{gather*}
This vector equation is called the parametric vector form of the solution set. Since \(x_3\) and \(x_4\) are allowed to be anything, this says that the solution set is the set of all linear combinations of \(\begin{pmatrix} 8 \\ -4 \\ 1 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix}\) and \(\begin{pmatrix} 7 \\ -3 \\ 0 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix}\text{.}\) In other words, the solution set is
\begin{gather*} \Span\left\{\begin{pmatrix} 8 \\ -4 \\ 1 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix},\;\begin{pmatrix} 7 \\ -3 \\ 0 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix}\right\}\text{.} \end{gather*}
