Example 3.5.31 Rotation.

Let \(T\colon\R^2\to\R^2\) be counterclockwise rotation by \(45^\circ\text{.}\) Is \(T\) invertible? If so, what is \(T\inv\text{?}\)
🔗

Solution.

In Example 18 of Section 3.3, we showed that the standard matrix for the counterclockwise rotation of the plane by an angle of \(\theta\) is
\begin{align*} \begin{pmatrix} \cos\theta \amp -\sin\theta \\ \sin\theta \amp \cos\theta \end{pmatrix}\text{.} \end{align*}
Therefore, the standard matrix \(A\) for \(T\) is
\begin{align*} A = \frac 1{\sqrt 2}\begin{pmatrix} 1 \amp -1 \\ 1 \amp 1 \end{pmatrix}\text{,} \end{align*}
where we have used the trigonometric identities
\begin{gather*} \cos(45^\circ) = \frac 1{\sqrt2} \qquad \sin(45^\circ) = \frac 1{\sqrt2}\text{.} \end{gather*}
The determinant of \(A\) is
\begin{gather*} \det(A) = \frac 1{\sqrt2}\cdot\frac 1{\sqrt2} - \frac 1{\sqrt2}\frac{-1}{\sqrt2} = \frac 12 + \frac 12 = 1\text{,} \end{gather*}
so the inverse is
\begin{align*} A\inv = \frac 1{\sqrt2}\begin{pmatrix} 1 \amp 1 \\ -1 \amp 1 \end{pmatrix}\text{.} \end{align*}
By the Theorem 29, \(T\) is invertible, and its inverse is the matrix transformation for \(A\inv\text{:}\)
\begin{align*} T\inv(x) = \frac 1{\sqrt2}\begin{pmatrix} 1 \amp 1 \\ -1 \amp 1 \end{pmatrix}x\text{.} \end{align*}
We recognize this as a clockwise rotation by \(45^\circ\text{,}\) using the trigonometric identities
\begin{gather*} \cos(-45^\circ) = \frac 1{\sqrt2} \qquad \sin(-45^\circ) = -\frac 1{\sqrt2}\text{.} \end{gather*}
