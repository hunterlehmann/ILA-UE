Example 2.8.6.

Let
\begin{gather*} v_1 = \begin{pmatrix} 2 \\ -1 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix} \quad v_2 = \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 0 \\ -1 \end{pmatrix}\text{.} \end{gather*}
These form a basis \(\cB\) for a plane \(V = \Span\{v_1,v_2\}\) in \(\R^3\text{.}\) We indicate the coordinate system defined by \(\cB\) by drawing lines parallel to the “\(v_1\)-axis” and “\(v_2\)-axis”:

We can see from the picture that the \(v_1\)-coordinate of \(\mh{u_1}\) is equal to \(1\text{,}\) as is the \(v_2\)-coordinate, so \([\textcolor{seq1}{u_1}]_\cB = {1\choose 1}.\) Similarly, we have
\begin{gather*} [\textcolor{seq2}{u_2}]_\cB = \begin{pmatrix} -1 \\ \frac 12 \end{pmatrix}\qquad [\textcolor{seq3}{u_3}]_\cB = \begin{pmatrix} \frac 32 \\ -\frac 12 \end{pmatrix}\qquad [\textcolor{seq5}{u_4}]_\cB = \begin{pmatrix} 0 \\ \frac 32 \end{pmatrix}\text{.} \end{gather*}
