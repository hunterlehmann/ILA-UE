Proposition 5.5.8.

Let
\begin{align*} A = \begin{pmatrix} a \amp -b \\ b \amp a \end{pmatrix} \end{align*}
be a rotation-scaling matrix. Then:
  1. \(A\) is a product of a rotation matrix
    \begin{align*} \begin{pmatrix} \cos\theta \amp -\sin\theta \\ \sin\theta \amp \cos\theta \end{pmatrix} \sptxt{with a scaling matrix} \begin{pmatrix} r \amp 0 \\ 0 \amp r \end{pmatrix}\text{.} \end{align*}
    🔗
    🔗
  2. The scaling factor \(r\) is
    \begin{gather*} r = \sqrt{\det(A)} = \sqrt{a^2+b^2}\text{.} \end{gather*}
    🔗
    🔗
  3. The rotation angle \(\theta\) is the counterclockwise angle from the positive \(x\)-axis to the vector \({a\choose b}\text{:}\) \usetikzlibrary{angles}
    🔗
    
    🔗
    🔗
  4. The eigenvalues of \(A\) are \(\lambda = a \pm bi.\)
    🔗
    🔗
🔗
🔗

Proof.
Set \(r = \sqrt{\det(A)} = \sqrt{a^2+b^2}\text{.}\) The point \((a/r, b/r)\) has the property that
\begin{gather*} \left(\frac ar\right)^2 + \left(\frac br\right)^2 = \frac{a^2+b^2}{r^2} = 1\text{.} \end{gather*}
In other words \((a/r,b/r)\) lies on the unit circle. Therefore, it has the form \((\cos\theta,\sin\theta)\text{,}\) where \(\theta\) is the counterclockwise angle from the positive \(x\)-axis to the vector \({a/r\choose b/r}\text{,}\) or since it is on the same line, to \({a\choose b}\text{:}\) \usetikzlibrary{angles}
🔗

🔗
It follows that
\begin{align*} A = r\begin{pmatrix} a/r \amp -b/r \\ b/r \amp a/r \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} r \amp 0 \\ 0 \amp r \end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix} \cos\theta \amp -\sin\theta \\ \sin\theta \amp \cos\theta \end{pmatrix}\text{,} \end{align*}
as desired.
🔗
For the last statement, we compute the eigenvalues of \(A\) as the roots of the characteristic polynomial:
\begin{gather*} \lambda = \frac{\Tr(A)\pm\sqrt{\Tr(A)^2-4\det(A)}}2 = \frac{2a\pm\sqrt{4a^2-4(a^2+b^2)}}2 = a\pm bi\text{.} \end{gather*}
