Note 2.3.6 Matrix Equations and Vector Equations.

Let \(v_1,v_2,\ldots,v_n\) and \(b\) be vectors in \(\R^m\text{.}\) Consider the vector equation
\begin{gather*} x_1v_1 + x_2v_2 + \cdots + x_nv_n = b\text{.} \end{gather*}
This is equivalent to the matrix equation \(Ax=b\text{,}\) where
\begin{align*} A = \begin{pmatrix} | \amp | \amp {} \amp | \\ v_1 \amp v_2 \amp \cdots \amp v_n \\ | \amp | \amp {} \amp | \end{pmatrix} \sptxt{and} x = \begin{pmatrix} x_1 \\ x_2 \\ \vdots \\ x_n \end{pmatrix}\text{.} \end{align*}
Conversely, if \(A\) is any \(m\times n\) matrix, then \(Ax=b\) is equivalent to the vector equation
\begin{gather*} x_1v_1 + x_2v_2 + \cdots + x_nv_n = b\text{,} \end{gather*}
where \(v_1,v_2,\ldots,v_n\) are the columns of \(A\text{,}\) and \(x_1,x_2,\ldots,x_n\) are the entries of \(x\text{.}\)
