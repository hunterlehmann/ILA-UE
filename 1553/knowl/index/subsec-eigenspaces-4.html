The above observation is important because it says that finding the eigenvectors for a given eigenvalue means solving a homogeneous system of equations. For instance, if
\begin{align*} A = \begin{pmatrix} 7 \amp 1 \amp 3 \\ -3 \amp 2 \amp -3 \\ -3 \amp -2 \amp -1 \end{pmatrix}\text{,} \end{align*}
then an eigenvector with eigenvalue \(\lambda\) is a nontrivial solution of the matrix equation
\begin{align*} \begin{pmatrix} 7 \amp 1 \amp 3 \\ -3 \amp 2 \amp -3 \\ -3 \amp -2 \amp -1 \end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix} x \\ y \\ z \end{pmatrix} = \lambda \begin{pmatrix} x \\ y \\ z \end{pmatrix}\text{.} \end{align*}
This translates to the system of equations
\begin{align*} \left\{\begin{alignedat}{4} 7x \amp {}+{} \amp y \amp {}+{} \amp 3z \amp {}={} \amp \lambda x \amp {} \\ -3x \amp {}+{} \amp 2y \amp {}-{} \amp 3z \amp {}={} \amp \lambda y \amp {} \\ -3x \amp {}-{} \amp 2y \amp {}-{} \amp z \amp {}={} \amp \lambda z \amp {} \end{alignedat}\right. \quad\xrightarrow{\phantom{MMM}}\quad \left\{\begin{alignedat}{6} (7 \amp {}-{} \amp \lambda)x \amp {}+{} \amp y \amp {}+{} \amp 3z \amp {} \amp {} \amp {}={} \amp 0 \amp {} \\ -3x \amp {}+{} \amp (2 \amp {}-{} \amp \lambda)y \amp {}-{} \amp 3z \amp {} \amp {} \amp {}={} \amp 0 \amp {} \\ -3x \amp {}-{} \amp 2y \amp {}+{} \amp ( \amp {}-{} \amp 1 \amp {}-{} \amp \lambda)z \amp {}={} \amp 0 \amp . \end{alignedat}\right\text{.} \end{align*}
This is the same as the homogeneous matrix equation
\begin{align*} \begin{pmatrix} 7-\lambda \amp 1 \amp 3 \\ -3 \amp 2-\lambda \amp -3 \\ -3 \amp -2 \amp -1-\lambda \end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix} x \\ y \\ z \end{pmatrix} = 0\text{,} \end{align*}
i.e., \((A-\lambda I_3)v = 0\text{.}\)
