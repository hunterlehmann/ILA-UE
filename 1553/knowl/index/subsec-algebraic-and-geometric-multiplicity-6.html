Example 5.3.44.

The shear matrix
\begin{align*} A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 \amp 1 \\ 0 \amp 1 \end{pmatrix} \end{align*}
has only one eigenvalue \(\lambda=1\text{.}\) The characteristic polynomial of \(A\) is \(f(\lambda) = (\lambda-1)^2\text{,}\) so \(1\) has algebraic multiplicity \(2\text{,}\) as it is a double root of \(f\text{.}\) On the other hand, we showed in Example 21 that the \(1\)-eigenspace of \(A\) is the \(x\)-axis, so the geometric multiplicity of \(1\) is equal to \(1\text{.}\) This matrix is not diagonalizable.
🔗
The identity matrix
\begin{align*} I_2 = \begin{pmatrix} 1 \amp 0 \\ 0 \amp 1 \end{pmatrix} \end{align*}
also has characteristic polynomial \((\lambda-1)^2\text{,}\) so the eigenvalue \(1\) has algebraic multiplicity \(2\text{.}\) Since every nonzero vector in \(\R^2\) is an eigenvector of \(I_2\) with eigenvalue \(1\text{,}\) the \(1\)-eigenspace is all of \(\R^2\text{,}\) so the geometric multiplicity is \(2\) as well. This matrix is diagonal.
