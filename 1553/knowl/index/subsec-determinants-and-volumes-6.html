Example 4.3.8 Area.

When \(A\) is a \(2\times 2\) matrix, its rows determine a parallelogram in \(\R^2\text{.}\) The “volume” of a region in \(\R^2\) is its area, so we obtain a formula for the area of a parallelogram: it is the determinant of the matrix whose rows are the vectors forming two adjacent sides of the parallelogram.

It is perhaps surprising that it is possible to compute the area of a parallelogram without trigonometry. It is a fun geometry problem to prove this formula by hand. [Hint: first think about the case when the first row of \(A\) lies on the \(x\)-axis.]
