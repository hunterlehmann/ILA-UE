Example 3.5.16 Dilation.

Let \(T\colon\R^2\to\R^2\) be dilation by a factor of \(3/2\text{:}\) that is, \(T(x) = 3/2x\text{.}\) Is \(T\) invertible? If so, what is \(T\inv\text{?}\)
🔗

Solution.

Let \(U\colon\R^2\to\R^2\) be dilation by a factor of \(2/3\text{:}\) that is, \(U(x) = 2/3x\text{.}\) Then
\begin{gather*} T\circ U(x) = T\biggl(\frac 23x\biggr) = \frac 32\cdot\frac 23x = x \end{gather*}
and
\begin{gather*} U\circ T(x) = U\biggl(\frac 32x\biggr) = \frac 23\cdot\frac 32x = x\text{.} \end{gather*}
Hence \(T\circ U = \Id_{\R^2}\) and \(U\circ T = \Id_{\R^2}\text{,}\) so \(T\) is invertible, with inverse \(U\text{.}\) In other words, shrinking by a factor of \(2/3\) undoes stretching by a factor of 3/2. \def\theo{\includegraphics[width=3cm]{theo6.jpg}} \begin{tikzpicture}[scale=1.3] \begin{scope} \node (theo1) at (0,0) {\theo}; \end{scope} \draw[->,opacity=.3] (-1.2,0) -- (1.2,0); \draw[->,opacity=.3] (0,-1.2) -- (0,1.2); \begin{scope}[xshift=4cm] \begin{scope} \node[scale=2/3] at (0,0) {\theo}; \node[minimum size={3cm+.2em}] (theo2) at (0,0) {}; \end{scope} \draw[->,opacity=.3] (-1.2,0) -- (1.2,0); \draw[->,opacity=.3] (0,-1.2) -- (0,1.2); \end{scope} \draw[->] (theo1.20) to["$U$", out=20, in=160] (theo2.160); \begin{scope}[xshift=8cm] \begin{scope} \node (theo3) at (0,0) {\theo}; \end{scope} \draw[->,opacity=.3] (-1.2,0) -- (1.2,0); \draw[->,opacity=.3] (0,-1.2) -- (0,1.2); \end{scope} \draw[->] (theo2.20) to["$T$", out=20, in=160] (theo3.160); \useasboundingbox (9.2,0); \end{tikzpicture}
