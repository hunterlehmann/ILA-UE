Example 2.7.7.

Example 6 implies that any basis for \(\R^n\) has \(n\) vectors in it. Let \(v_1,v_2,\ldots,v_n\) be vectors in \(\R^n\text{,}\) and let \(A\) be the \(n\times n\) matrix with columns \(v_1,v_2,\ldots,v_n\text{.}\)
  1. To say that \(\{v_1,v_2,\ldots,v_n\}\) spans \(\R^n\) means that \(A\) has a pivot position in every row: see Theorem 16 of Section 2.3.
    🔗
    🔗
  2. To say that \(\{v_1,v_2,\ldots,v_n\}\) is linearly independent means that \(A\) has a pivot position in every column: see Recipe 9 of Section 2.5.
    🔗
    🔗
Since \(A\) is a square matrix, it has a pivot in every row if and only if it has a pivot in every column. We will see in Section 3.5 that the above two conditions are equivalent to the invertibility of the matrix \(A\text{.}\)
