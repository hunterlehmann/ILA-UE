Remark 6.1.14 Angle between two vectors.

More generally, the law of cosines gives a formula for the angle \(\alpha\) between two nonzero vectors:
\begin{align*} 2\|x\|\|y\|\cos(\alpha) \amp= \|x\|^2 + \|y\|^2 - \|y-x\|^2\\ \amp= x\cdot x + y\cdot y - (y-x)\cdot (y-x)\\ \amp= x\cdot x + y\cdot y - y\cdot y - x\cdot x + 2x\cdot y\\ \amp= 2x\cdot y\\ \amp\quad\implies \alpha = \cos\inv\left(\frac{x\cdot y}{\|x\|\|y\|}\right)\text{.} \end{align*}
In higher dimensions, we take this to be the definition of the angle between \(x\) and \(y\text{.}\)
