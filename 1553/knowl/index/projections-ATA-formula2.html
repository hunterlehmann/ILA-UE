Corollary 6.3.29.

Let \(A\) be an \(m \times n\) matrix with linearly independent columns and let \(W = \Col(A)\text{.}\) Then the \(n\times n\) matrix \(A^TA\) is invertible, and for all vectors \(x\) in \(\R^m\text{,}\) we have
\begin{gather*} x_W = A(A^TA)\inv A^Tx\text{.} \end{gather*}
🔗
🔗

Proof.
We will show that \(\Nul(A^TA)=\{0\}\text{,}\) which implies invertibility by the Theorem 31 of Section 5.1. Suppose that \(A^TAc = 0\text{.}\) Then \(A^TAc = A^T0\text{,}\) so \(0_W = Ac\) by the Theorem 14. But \(0_W = 0\) (the orthogonal decomposition of the zero vector is just \(0 = 0 + 0)\text{,}\) so \(Ac = 0\text{,}\) and therefore \(c\) is in \(\Nul(A)\text{.}\) Since the columns of \(A\) are linearly independent, we have \(c=0\text{,}\) so \(\Nul(A^TA)=0\text{,}\) as desired.
🔗
Let \(x\) be a vector in \(\R^n\) and let \(c\) be a solution of \(A^TAc = A^Tx\text{.}\) Then \(c = (A^TA)\inv A^Tx\text{,}\) so \(x_W =Ac = A(A^TA)\inv A^Tx\text{.}\)
