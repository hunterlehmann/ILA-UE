Theorem 6.4.11.

Let \(A\) be an \(m\times n\) matrix and let \(b\) be a vector in \(\R^m\text{.}\) The following are equivalent:
  1. \(Ax=b\) has a unique least-squares solution.
    🔗
    🔗
  2. The columns of \(A\) are linearly independent.
    🔗
    🔗
  3. \(A^TA\) is invertible.
    🔗
    🔗
In this case, the least-squares solution is
\begin{gather*} \hat x = (A^TA)\inv A^Tb\text{.} \end{gather*}
🔗
🔗

Proof.
The set of least-squares solutions of \(Ax=b\) is the solution set of the consistent equation \(A^TAx=A^Tb\text{,}\) which is a translate of the solution set of the homogeneous equation \(A^TAx=0\text{.}\) Since \(A^TA\) is a square matrix, the equivalence of 1 and 3 follows from the Theorem 31 of Section 5.1. The set of least squares-solutions is also the solution set of the consistent equation \(Ax = b_{\Col(A)}\text{,}\) which has a unique solution if and only if the columns of \(A\) are linearly independent by Recipe 9 of Section 2.5.
