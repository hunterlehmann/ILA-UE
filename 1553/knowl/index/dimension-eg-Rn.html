Example 2.7.6 The standard basis of \(\R^n\).

One shows exactly as in the above Example 4 that the standard coordinate vectors
\begin{gather*} e_1=\begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 0 \\ \vdots \\ 0 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix},\quad e_2=\begin{pmatrix} 0 \\ 1 \\ \vdots \\ 0 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix}, \quad\ldots,\quad e_{n-1}=\begin{pmatrix} 0 \\ 0 \\ \vdots \\ 1 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix},\quad e_n=\begin{pmatrix} 0 \\ 0 \\ \vdots \\ 0 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix} \end{gather*}
form a basis for \(\R^n\text{.}\) This is sometimes known as the standard basis.
🔗
In particular, \(\R^n\) has dimension \(n\text{.}\)
