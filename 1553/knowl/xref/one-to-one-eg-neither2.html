Example 3.2.24 A matrix transformation that is not onto.

Let
\begin{align*} A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 \amp -1 \amp 2 \\ -2 \amp 2 \amp -4 \end{pmatrix}\text{,} \end{align*}
and define \(T\colon\R^3\to\R^2\) by \(T(x) = Ax\text{.}\) Is \(T\) onto? If not, find a vector \(b\) in \(\R^2\) such that \(T(x)=b\) has no solution.
🔗

Solution.

The reduced row echelon form of \(A\) is
\begin{align*} \begin{pmatrix} 1 \amp -1 \amp 2 \\ 0 \amp 0 \amp 0 \end{pmatrix}\text{.} \end{align*}
There is not a pivot in every row, so \(T\) is not onto. The range of \(T\) is the column space of \(A\text{,}\) which is equal to
\begin{gather*} \Span\left\{\begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ -2 \end{pmatrix},\;\begin{pmatrix} -1 \\ 2 \end{pmatrix},\;\begin{pmatrix} 2 \\ -4 \end{pmatrix}\right\} = \Span\left\{\begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ -2 \end{pmatrix}\right\}\text{,} \end{gather*}
since all three columns of \(A\) are collinear. Therefore, any vector not on the line through \(1\choose-2\) is not in the range of \(T\text{.}\) For instance, if \(b={1\choose 1}\) then \(T(x)=b\) has no solution.
