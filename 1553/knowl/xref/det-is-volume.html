Theorem 4.3.6 Determinants and volumes.

Let \(v_1,v_2,\ldots,v_n\) be vectors in \(\R^n\text{,}\) let \(P\) be the parallelepiped determined by these vectors, and let \(A\) be the matrix with rows \(v_1,v_2,\ldots,v_n\text{.}\) Then the absolute value of the determinant of \(A\) is the volume of \(P\text{:}\)
\begin{gather*} |\det(A)| = \vol(P)\text{.} \end{gather*}
🔗
🔗

Proof.
Since the four defining properties characterize the determinant, they also characterize the absolute value of the determinant. Explicitly, \(|\det|\) is a function on square matrices which satisfies these properties:
  1. Doing a row replacement on \(A\) does not change \(|\det(A)|\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
  2. Scaling a row of \(A\) by a scalar \(c\) multiplies \(|\det(A)|\) by \(|c|\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
  3. Swapping two rows of a matrix does not change \(|\det(A)|\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
  4. The determinant of the identity matrix \(I_n\) is equal to \(1\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
The absolute value of the determinant is the only such function: indeed, by Recipe 5 of Section 4.1, if you do some number of row operations on \(A\) to obtain a matrix \(B\) in row echelon form, then
\begin{gather*} |\det(A)| = \left|\frac{\text{(product of the diagonal entries of $B$)}} {\text{(product of scaling factors used)}}\right|\text{.} \end{gather*}
🔗
For a square matrix \(A\text{,}\) we abuse notation and let \(\vol(A)\) denote the volume of the paralellepiped determined by the rows of \(A\text{.}\) Then we can regard \(\vol\) as a function from the set of square matrices to the real numbers. We will show that \(\vol\) also satisfies the above four properties.
  1. For simplicity, we consider a row replacement of the form \(R_n = R_n + cR_i\text{.}\) The volume of a paralellepiped is the volume of its base, times its height: here the “base” is the paralellepiped determined by \(v_1,v_2,\ldots,v_{n-1}\text{,}\) and the “height” is the perpendicular distance of \(v_n\) from the base.
    🔗
    
    🔗
    Translating \(v_n\) by a multiple of \(v_i\) moves \(v_n\) in a direction parallel to the base. This changes neither the base nor the height! Thus, \(\vol(A)\) is unchanged by row replacements. \usetikzlibrary{math}
    🔗
    
    🔗
    🔗
  2. For simplicity, we consider a row scale of the form \(R_n = cR_n.\) This scales the length of \(v_n\) by a factor of \(|c|\text{,}\) which also scales the perpendicular distance of \(v_n\) from the base by a factor of \(|c|\text{.}\) Thus, \(\vol(A)\) is scaled by \(|c|\text{.}\) \usetikzlibrary{math}
    🔗
    
    🔗
    🔗
  3. Swapping two rows of \(A\) just reorders the vectors \(v_1,v_2,\ldots,v_n\text{,}\) hence has no effect on the parallelepiped determined by those vectors. Thus, \(\vol(A)\) is unchanged by row swaps.
    🔗
    
    🔗
    🔗
  4. The rows of the identity matrix \(I_n\) are the standard coordinate vectors \(e_1,e_2,\ldots,e_n\text{.}\) The associated paralellepiped is the unit cube, which has volume \(1\text{.}\) Thus, \(\vol(I_n) = 1\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
Since \(|\det|\) is the only function satisfying these properties, we have
\begin{gather*} \vol(P) = \vol(A) = |\det(A)|\text{.} \end{gather*}
This completes the proof.
