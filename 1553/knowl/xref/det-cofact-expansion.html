Theorem 4.2.3 Cofactor expansion.

Let \(A\) be an \(n\times n\) matrix with entries \(a_{ij}\text{.}\)
  1. For any \(i = 1,2,\ldots,n\text{,}\) we have
    \begin{gather*} \det(A) = \sum_{j=1}^n a_{ij}C_{ij} = a_{i1}C_{i1} + a_{i2}C_{i2} + \cdots + a_{in}C_{in}\text{.} \end{gather*}
    This is called cofactor expansion along the \(i\)th row.
    🔗
    🔗
  2. For any \(j = 1,2,\ldots,n\text{,}\) we have
    \begin{gather*} \det(A) = \sum_{i=1}^n a_{ij}C_{ij} = a_{1j}C_{1j} + a_{2j}C_{2j} + \cdots + a_{nj}C_{nj}\text{.} \end{gather*}
    This is called cofactor expansion along the \(j\)th column.
    🔗
    🔗
🔗
🔗

Proof.
First we will prove that cofactor expansion along the first column computes the determinant. Define a function \(d\colon\{n\times n\text{ matrices}\}\to\R\) by
\begin{gather*} d(A) = \sum_{i=1}^n (-1)^{i+1} a_{i1}\det(A_{i1})\text{.} \end{gather*}
We want to show that \(d(A) = \det(A)\text{.}\) Instead of showing that \(d\) satisfies the four Definition 1 of Section 4.1, we will prove that it satsifies the three Remark 29 of Section 4.1, which were shown to be equivalent.
  1. We claim that \(d\) is multilinear in the rows of \(A\text{.}\) Let \(A\) be the matrix with rows \(v_1,v_2,\ldots,v_{i-1},v+w,v_{i+1},\ldots,v_n\text{:}\)
    \begin{align*} A = \begin{pmatrix} a_{11} \amp a_{12} \amp a_{13} \\ b_1+c_1 \amp b_2+c_2 \amp b_3+c_3 \\ a_{31} \amp a_{32} \amp a_{33} \end{pmatrix}\text{.} \end{align*}
    Here we let \(b_i\) and \(c_i\) be the entries of \(v\) and \(w\text{,}\) respectively. Let \(B\) and \(C\) be the matrices with rows \(v_1,v_2,\ldots,v_{i-1},v,v_{i+1},\ldots,v_n\) and \(v_1,v_2,\ldots,v_{i-1},w,v_{i+1},\ldots,v_n\text{,}\) respectively:
    \begin{align*} B = \begin{pmatrix} a_{11} \amp a_{12} \amp a_{13} \\ b_1 \amp b_2 \amp b_3 \\ a_{31} \amp a_{32} \amp a_{33} \end{pmatrix} \qquad C = \begin{pmatrix} a_{11} \amp a_{12} \amp a_{13} \\ c_1 \amp c_2 \amp c_3 \\ a_{31} \amp a_{32} \amp a_{33} \end{pmatrix}\text{.} \end{align*}
    We wish to show \(d(A) = d(B) + d(C)\text{.}\) For \(i'\neq i\text{,}\) the \((i',1)\)-cofactor of \(A\) is the sum of the \((i',1)\)-cofactors of \(B\) and \(C\text{,}\) by multilinearity of the determinants of \((n-1)\times(n-1)\) matrices:
    \begin{align*} (-1)^{3+1}\det(A_{31}) \amp= (-1)^{3+1}\det\begin{pmatrix} a_{12} \amp a_{13} \\ b_2+c_2 \amp b_3+c_3 \end{pmatrix}\\ \amp= (-1)^{3+1}\det\begin{pmatrix} a_{12} \amp a_{13} \\ b_2 \amp b_3 \end{pmatrix} + (-1)^{3+1}\det\begin{pmatrix} a_{12} \amp a_{13} \\ c_2 \amp c_3 \end{pmatrix}\\ \amp= (-1)^{3+1}\det(B_{31}) + (-1)^{3+1}\det(C_{31})\text{.} \end{align*}
    On the other hand, the \((i,1)\)-cofactors of \(A,B,\) and \(C\) are all the same:
    \begin{align*} (-1)^{2+1} \det(A_{21}) \amp= (-1)^{2+1} \det\begin{pmatrix} a_{12} \amp a_{13} \\ a_{32} \amp a_{33} \end{pmatrix}\\ \amp= (-1)^{2+1} \det(B_{21}) = (-1)^{2+1} \det(C_{21})\text{.} \end{align*}
    Now we compute
    \begin{align*} d(A) \amp= (-1)^{i+1} (b_i + c_i)\det(A_{i1}) + \sum_{i'\neq i} (-1)^{i'+1} a_{i1}\det(A_{i'1})\\ \amp= (-1)^{i+1} b_i\det(B_{i1}) + (-1)^{i+1} c_i\det(C_{i1})\\ \amp\qquad\qquad+ \sum_{i'\neq i} (-1)^{i'+1} a_{i1}\bigl(\det(B_{i'1}) + \det(C_{i'1})\bigr)\\ \amp= \left[(-1)^{i+1} b_i\det(B_{i1}) + \sum_{i'\neq i} (-1)^{i'+1} a_{i1}\det(B_{i'1})\right]\\ \amp\qquad\qquad+ \left[(-1)^{i+1} c_i\det(C_{i1}) + \sum_{i'\neq i} (-1)^{i'+1} a_{i1}\det(C_{i'1})\right]\\ \amp= d(B) + d(C)\text{,} \end{align*}
    as desired. This shows that \(d(A)\) satisfies the first defining property in the rows of \(A\text{.}\)
    🔗
    We still have to show that \(d(A)\) satisfies the second defining property in the rows of \(A\text{.}\) Let \(B\) be the matrix obtained by scaling the \(i\)th row of \(A\) by a factor of \(c\text{:}\)
    \begin{align*} A = \begin{pmatrix} a_{11} \amp a_{12} \amp a_{13} \\ a_{21} \amp a_{22} \amp a_{23} \\ a_{31} \amp a_{32} \amp a_{33} \end{pmatrix} \qquad B = \begin{pmatrix} a_{11} \amp a_{12} \amp a_{13} \\ ca_{21} \amp ca_{22} \amp ca_{23} \\ a_{31} \amp a_{32} \amp a_{33} \end{pmatrix}\text{.} \end{align*}
    We wish to show that \(d(B) = c\,d(A)\text{.}\) For \(i'\neq i\text{,}\) the \((i',1)\)-cofactor of \(B\) is \(c\) times the \((i',1)\)-cofactor of \(A\text{,}\) by multilinearity of the determinants of \((n-1)\times(n-1)\)-matrices:
    \begin{align*} (-1)^{3+1}\det(B_{31}) \amp= (-1)^{3+1}\det\begin{pmatrix} a_{12} \amp a_{13} \\ ca_{22} \amp ca_{23} \end{pmatrix}\\ \amp= (-1)^{3+1}\cdot c\det\begin{pmatrix} a_{12} \amp a_{13} \\ a_{22} \amp a_{23} \end{pmatrix} = (-1)^{3+1}\cdot c\det(A_{31})\text{.} \end{align*}
    On the other hand, the \((i,1)\)-cofactors of \(A\) and \(B\) are the same:
    \begin{align*} (-1)^{2+1} \det(B_{21}) = (-1)^{2+1} \det\begin{pmatrix} a_{12} \amp a_{13} \\ a_{32} \amp a_{33} \end{pmatrix} = (-1)^{2+1} \det(A_{21})\text{.} \end{align*}
    Now we compute
    \begin{align*} d(B) \amp= (-1)^{i+1}ca_{i1}\det(B_{i1}) + \sum_{i'\neq i}(-1)^{i'+1} a_{i'1}\det(B_{i'1})\\ \amp= (-1)^{i+1}ca_{i1}\det(A_{i1}) + \sum_{i'\neq i}(-1)^{i'+1} a_{i'1}\cdot c\det(A_{i'1})\\ \amp= c\left[(-1)^{i+1}ca_{i1}\det(A_{i1}) + \sum_{i'\neq i}(-1)^{i'+1} a_{i'1} \det(A_{i'1})\right]\\ \amp= c\,d(A)\text{,} \end{align*}
    as desired. This completes the proof that \(d(A)\) is multilinear in the rows of \(A\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
  2. Now we show that \(d(A) = 0\) if \(A\) has two identical rows. Suppose that rows \(i_1,i_2\) of \(A\) are identical, with \(i_1 \lt i_2\text{:}\)
    \begin{align*} A = \begin{pmatrix} a_{11} \amp a_{12} \amp a_{13} \amp a_{14} \\ a_{21} \amp a_{22} \amp a_{23} \amp a_{24} \\ a_{31} \amp a_{32} \amp a_{33} \amp a_{34} \\ a_{11} \amp a_{12} \amp a_{13} \amp a_{14} \end{pmatrix}\text{.} \end{align*}
    If \(i\neq i_1,i_2\) then the \((i,1)\)-cofactor of \(A\) is equal to zero, since \(A_{i1}\) is an \((n-1)\times(n-1)\) matrix with identical rows:
    \begin{align*} (-1)^{2+1}\det(A_{21}) = (-1)^{2+1} \det\begin{pmatrix} a_{12} \amp a_{13} \amp a_{14} \\ a_{32} \amp a_{33} \amp a_{34} \\ a_{12} \amp a_{13} \amp a_{14} \end{pmatrix} = 0\text{.} \end{align*}
    The \((i_1,1)\)-minor can be transformed into the \((i_2,1)\)-minor using \(i_2 - i_1 - 1\) row swaps:
    🔗
    
    🔗
    Therefore,
    \begin{gather*} (-1)^{i_1+1}\det(A_{i_11}) = (-1)^{i_1+1}\cdot(-1)^{i_2 - i_1 - 1}\det(A_{i_21}) = -(-1)^{i_2+1}\det(A_{i_21})\text{.} \end{gather*}
    The two remaining cofactors cancel out, so \(d(A) = 0\text{,}\) as desired.
    🔗
    🔗
  3. It remains to show that \(d(I_n) = 1\text{.}\) The first is the only one nonzero term in the cofactor expansion of the identity:
    \begin{gather*} d(I_n) = 1\cdot(-1)^{1+1}\det(I_{n-1}) = 1\text{.} \end{gather*}
    🔗
    🔗
This proves that \(\det(A) = d(A)\text{,}\) i.e., that cofactor expansion along the first column computes the determinant.
🔗
Now we show that cofactor expansion along the \(j\)th column also computes the determinant. By performing \(j-1\) column swaps, one can move the \(j\)th column of a matrix to the first column, keeping the other columns in order. For example, here we move the third column to the first, using two column swaps:

Let \(B\) be the matrix obtained by moving the \(j\)th column of \(A\) to the first column in this way. Then the \((i,j)\) minor \(A_{ij}\) is equal to the \((i,1)\) minor \(B_{i1}\text{,}\) since deleting the \(i\)th column of \(A\) is the same as deleting the first column of \(B\text{.}\) By construction, the \((i,j)\)-entry \(a_{ij}\) of \(A\) is equal to the \((i,1)\)-entry \(b_{i1}\) of \(B\text{.}\) Since we know that we can compute determinants by expanding along the first column, we have
\begin{gather*} \det(B) = \sum_{i=1}^n (-1)^{i+1} b_{i1}\det(B_{i1}) = \sum_{i=1}^n (-1)^{i+1} a_{ij}\det(A_{ij})\text{.} \end{gather*}
Since \(B\) was obtained from \(A\) by performing \(j-1\) column swaps, we have
\begin{align*} \det(A) = (-1)^{j-1}\det(B) \amp= (-1)^{j-1}\sum_{i=1}^n (-1)^{i+1} a_{ij}\det(A_{ij})\\ \amp= \sum_{i=1}^n (-1)^{i+j} a_{ij}\det(A_{ij})\text{.} \end{align*}
This proves that cofactor expansion along the \(i\)th column computes the determinant of \(A\text{.}\)
🔗
By the Proposition 25 of Section 4.1, the cofactor expansion along the \(i\)th row of \(A\) is the same as the cofactor expansion along the \(i\)th column of \(A^T\text{.}\) Again by the transpose property, we have \(\det(A)=\det(A^T)\text{,}\) so expanding cofactors along a row also computes the determinant.
