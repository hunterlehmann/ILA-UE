Example 3.1.31 Questions about a [non-matrix] transformation.

Define a transformation \(T\colon\R^2\to\R^3\) by the formula
\begin{gather*} T\begin{pmatrix} x \\ y \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} \ln(x) \\ \cos(y) \\ \ln(x) \end{pmatrix}\text{.} \end{gather*}
  1. Evaluate \(T(u)\) for \(u=\begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ \pi \end{pmatrix}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
  2. Let
    \begin{gather*} b = \begin{pmatrix} 7 \\ 1 \\ 7 \end{pmatrix}\text{.} \end{gather*}
    Find a vector \(v\) in \(\R^2\) such that \(T(v)=b\text{.}\) Is there more than one?
    🔗
    🔗
  3. Does there exist a vector \(w\) in \(\R^3\) such that there is more than one \(v\) in \(\R^2\) with \(T(v)=w\text{?}\)
    🔗
    🔗
  4. Find a vector \(w\) in \(\R^3\) which is not in the range of \(T\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
🔗
Note: we asked (almost) the exact same questions about a matrix transformation in Example 30. The point of this example is to illustrate the fact that the questions make sense for a transformation that has no hope of coming from a matrix. In this case, these questions do not translate into questions about a matrix; they have to be answered in some other way.
🔗

Solution.

  1. We evaluate \(T(u)\) using the defining formula:
    \begin{gather*} T\begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ \pi \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} \ln(1) \\ \cos(\pi) \\ \ln(1) \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 0 \\ -1 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix}\text{.} \end{gather*}
    🔗
    🔗
  2. We have
    \begin{gather*} T\begin{pmatrix} e^7 \\ 2\pi \\ n \\ e^7 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} \ln(e^7) \\ \cos(2\pi \\ n) \\ \ln(e^7) \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 7 \\ 1 \\ 7 \end{pmatrix} \end{gather*}
    for any whole number \(n\text{.}\) Hence there are infinitely many such vectors.
    🔗
    🔗
  3. The vector \(b\) from the previous part is an example of such a vector.
    🔗
    🔗
  4. Since \(\cos(y)\) is always between \(-1\) and \(1\text{,}\) the vector
    \begin{gather*} w = \begin{pmatrix} 0 \\ 2 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix} \end{gather*}
    is not in the range of \(T\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
🔗
🔗
