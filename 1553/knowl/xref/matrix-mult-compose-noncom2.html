Example 3.4.23 Non-commutative multiplication of matrices.

Consider the matrices
\begin{align*} A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 \amp 1 \\ 0 \amp 1 \end{pmatrix} \sptxt{and} B = \begin{pmatrix} 1 \amp 0 \\ 1 \amp 1 \end{pmatrix}\text{,} \end{align*}
as in Example 13. The matrix \(AB\) is
\begin{align*} \begin{pmatrix} 1 \amp 1 \\ 0 \amp 1 \end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix} 1 \amp 0 \\ 1 \amp 1 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 2 \amp 1 \\ 1 \amp 1 \end{pmatrix}\text{,} \end{align*}
whereas the matrix \(BA\) is
\begin{align*} \begin{pmatrix} 1 \amp 0 \\ 1 \amp 1 \end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix} 1 \amp 1 \\ 0 \amp 1 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 \amp 1 \\ 1 \amp 2 \end{pmatrix}\text{.} \end{align*}
In particular, we have
\begin{gather*} AB \neq BA\text{.} \end{gather*}
And so matrix multiplication is not always commutative. It is not a coincidence that this example agrees with Example 13; we are about to see that multiplication of matrices corresponds to composition of transformations.
