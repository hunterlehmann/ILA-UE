Example 5.5.7.

Continuing with the Example 2, the matrix
\begin{align*} A = \begin{pmatrix} .3 \amp .4 \amp .5 \\ .3 \amp .4 \amp .3 \\ .4 \amp .2 \amp .2 \end{pmatrix} \end{align*}
is a positive stochastic matrix. The fact that the columns sum to \(1\) says that all of the movies rented from a particular kiosk must be returned to some other kiosk (remember that every customer returns their movie the next day). For instance, the first column says:
🔗
Of the movies rented from kiosk \(1\text{,}\)
\begin{align*} 30\% \amp\text{ will be returned to kiosk } 1\\ 30\% \amp\text{ will be returned to kiosk } 2\\ 40\% \amp\text{ will be returned to kiosk } 3\text{.} \end{align*}
The sum is \(100\%\text{,}\) as all of the movies are returned to one of the three kiosks.
🔗
🔗
The matrix \(A\) represents the change of state from one day to the next:
\begin{gather*} \begin{pmatrix} x_{t+1} \\ y_{t+1} \\ z_{t+1} \end{pmatrix} = A\begin{pmatrix} x_t \\ y_t \\ z_t \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} .3x_t+.4y_t+.5z_t \\ .3x_t+.4y_t+.3z_t \\ .4x_t+.2y_t+.2z_t \end{pmatrix}\text{.} \end{gather*}
If we sum the entries of \(v_{t+1}\text{,}\) we obtain
\begin{align*} \amp(.3x_t+.4y_t+.5z_t)+ (.3x_t+.4y_t+.3z_t)+ (.4x_t+.2y_t+.2z_t)\\ \amp\qquad=(.3+.3+.4)x_t + (.4+.4+.2)y_t + (.5+.3+.2)z_t\\ \amp\qquad= x_t + y_t + z_t\text{.} \end{align*}
This says that the total number of copies of Prognosis Negative in the three kiosks does not change from day to day, as we expect.
