Example 3.3.18 The matrix of a rotation.

Define \(T\colon\R^2\to\R^2\) by
\begin{gather*} T(x) = \text{ the vector $x$ rotated counterclockwise by the angle $\theta$}\text{.} \end{gather*}
Find the standard matrix for \(T\text{.}\)
🔗

Solution.

The columns of \(A\) are obtained by evaluating \(T\) on the standard coordinate vectors \(e_1,e_2\text{.}\) In order to compute the entries of \(T(e_1)\) and \(T(e_2)\text{,}\) we have to do some trigonometry. \usetikzlibrary{math}
🔗

🔗
We see from the picture that
\begin{align*} \left.\begin{aligned} T(e_1) \amp= \begin{pmatrix} \color{seq2}\cos\theta \\ \color{seq1}\sin\theta \end{pmatrix} \\ T(e_2) \amp= \begin{pmatrix} -\color{seq1}\sin\theta \\ \color{seq2}\cos\theta \end{pmatrix} \end{aligned}\right\} \implies A = \begin{pmatrix} \color{seq2}\cos\theta \amp -\color{seq1}\sin\theta \\ \color{seq1}\sin\theta \amp \color{seq2}\cos\theta \end{pmatrix} \end{align*}
