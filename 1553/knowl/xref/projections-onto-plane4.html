Example 6.3.36.

Continuing with the above Example 34, we showed that
\begin{align*} B = \frac 13\begin{pmatrix} 2 \amp 1 \amp -1 \\ 1 \amp 2 \amp 1 \\ -1 \amp 1 \amp 2 \end{pmatrix} \end{align*}
is the standard matrix of the orthogonal projection onto
\begin{gather*} W = \Span\left\{\begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 0 \\ -1 \end{pmatrix},\;\begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 1 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix}\right\}\text{.} \end{gather*}
One can verify by hand that \(B^2=B\) (try it!). We compute \(W^\perp\) as the null space of
\begin{align*} \begin{pmatrix} 1 \amp 0 \amp -1 \\ 1 \amp 1 \amp 0 \end{pmatrix} \rref \begin{pmatrix} 1 \amp 0 \amp -1 \\ 0 \amp 1 \amp 1 \end{pmatrix}\text{.} \end{align*}
The free variable is \(x_3\text{,}\) and the parametric form is \(x_1 = x_3,\,x_2 = -x_3\text{,}\) so that
\begin{gather*} W^\perp = \Span\left\{\begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ -1 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix}\right\}\text{.} \end{gather*}
It follows that \(B\) has eigenvectors
\begin{gather*} \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 0 \\ -1 \end{pmatrix},\qquad \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 1 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix},\qquad \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ -1 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix} \end{gather*}
with eigenvalues \(1,1,0\text{,}\) respectively, so that
\begin{align*} B = \begin{pmatrix} 1 \amp 1 \amp 1 \\ 0 \amp 1 \amp -1 \\ -1 \amp 0 \amp 1 \end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix} 1 \amp 0 \amp 0 \\ 0 \amp 1 \amp 0 \\ 0 \amp 0 \amp 0 \end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix} 1 \amp 1 \amp 1 \\ 0 \amp 1 \amp -1 \\ -1 \amp 0 \amp 1 \end{pmatrix}\inv\text{.} \end{align*}
