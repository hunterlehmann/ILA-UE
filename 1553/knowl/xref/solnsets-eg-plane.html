Example 2.4.9 The solution set is a plane.

Compute the parametric vector form of the solution set of \(Ax=0\text{,}\) where
\begin{align*} A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 \amp -1 \amp 2 \\ -2 \amp 2 \amp -4 \end{pmatrix}\text{.} \end{align*}
🔗

Solution.

We row reduce (without augmenting, as suggested in the above Example 4):
\begin{align*} \begin{pmatrix} 1 \amp -1 \amp 2 \\ -2 \amp 2 \amp -4 \end{pmatrix} \quad\xrightarrow{\text{RREF}}\quad \begin{pmatrix} 1 \amp -1 \amp 2 \\ 0 \amp 0 \amp 0 \end{pmatrix}\text{.} \end{align*}
This corresponds to the single equation \(x_1 - x_2 + 2x_3 = 0\text{.}\) We write the parametric form including the redundant equations \(x_2=x_2\) and \(x_3=x_3\text{:}\)
\begin{align*} \left\{\begin{alignedat}{2} x_1 \amp {}={} \amp x_2 - 2x_3 \amp {} \\ x_2 \amp {}={} \amp x_2 \amp {} \\ x_3 \amp {}={} \amp x_3 \amp . \end{alignedat}\right. \end{align*}
We turn these into a single vector equation:
\begin{gather*} x = \begin{pmatrix} x_1 \\ x_2 \\ x_3 \end{pmatrix} = x_2\begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 1 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix} + x_3\begin{pmatrix} -2 \\ 0 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix}\text{.} \end{gather*}
This is the parametric vector form of the solution set. Since \(x_2\) and \(x_3\) are allowed to be anything, this says that the solution set is the set of all linear combinations of \(\begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 1 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix}\) and \(\begin{pmatrix} -2 \\ 0 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix}\text{.}\) In other words, the solution set is
\begin{gather*} \Span\left\{\begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 1 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix},\;\begin{pmatrix} -2 \\ 0 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix}\right\}\text{.} \end{gather*}
