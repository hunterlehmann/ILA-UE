The matrix of cofactors is sometimes called the adjugate matrix of \(A\text{,}\) and is denoted \(\adj(A)\text{:}\)
\begin{align*} \adj(A) = \begin{pmatrix} C_{11} \amp C_{21} \amp \cdots \amp C_{n-1,1} \amp C_{n1} \\ C_{12} \amp C_{22} \amp \cdots \amp C_{n-1,2} \amp C_{n2} \\ \vdots \amp \vdots \amp \ddots \amp \vdots \amp \vdots \\ C_{1,n-1} \amp C_{2,n-1} \amp \cdots \amp C_{n-1,n-1} \amp C_{n,n-1} \\ C_{1n} \amp C_{2n} \amp \cdots \amp C_{n-1,n} \amp C_{nn} \end{pmatrix}\text{.} \end{align*}
Note that the \((i,j)\) cofactor \(C_{ij}\) goes in the \((j,i)\) entry the adjugate matrix, not the \((i,j)\) entry: the adjugate matrix is the transpose of the cofactor matrix.
